Why most AI pilots stall

The tools are not the problem. The workflow is.

Almost every organization I talk to has already tried ChatGPT. Somebody on staff used it for a week, got a few impressive answers and a few embarrassing ones, and quietly went back to doing it the old way.

That is not a tool failure. It is a workflow failure. A useful AI process needs a defined input, a tested prompt, a place the output goes, a person who owns it, and a check that catches it when it is wrong. Build those five things and the same task stops eating four hours a week. Skip them and you get a novelty.

  • Verified, not vibes. Every workflow I build has a way to check the output against a source.
  • Owned by your team. Documented and taught, so it survives after I leave.
  • Honest about limits. Some of your work should not go near a language model, and I will tell you which parts.
