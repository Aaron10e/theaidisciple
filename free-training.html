How do I get the free AI training?

Send a request through the contact form on theaidisciple.com and Aaron Tenney emails you a private link to the free AI training videos personally. There is no fee, no subscription and no sales call attached to it. The only thing asked for is a name and an email address so the link can be sent and so questions can be answered afterwards.

The course is AI basics for total beginners: what AI actually is, how to get a usable answer out of it, how to catch it when it is confidently wrong, and what should never be typed into a chat window in the first place.

Why give it away

Because most people are stuck at the first step

Nearly everyone I meet has heard that AI is going to change their work, and nearly nobody has been shown how to use it for anything more useful than a limerick. Then they read a headline about AI making things up, decide it cannot be trusted, and stop.

That gap is not going to be closed by a paid course that most people will never buy. So the beginner material is free, to anyone, regardless of whether they will ever be a client. If it turns out you want help building something real afterwards, that is what the consulting side is for. If it does not, you still keep the training.

The only thing I ask is that you come through the contact form rather than a download button. I send the link myself, which means I can answer your questions afterwards and point you at the right next thing instead of dropping you into an autoresponder.

The course

AI basics for total beginners

Six short video lessons. Plain English, no jargon, nothing you need to install. Watch them in an evening or one a week — the link does not expire.

01

What AI actually is

What a language model is really doing when it answers you, in terms that do not require a math degree — and why understanding that one thing changes how you use it.

02

Your first hour

Opening a chat tool for the first time, what the buttons do, which free tool to start with, and the first three things worth trying with your own work.

03

Asking so you get something usable

The difference between a question that produces vague filler and one that produces a draft you would actually send. Context, examples, format and role — with real before-and-after examples.

04

Catching a confident mistake

Why AI invents facts, what a hallucination looks like when it is dressed up in professional language, and a simple habit for checking an answer before you rely on it.

05

What never to type into it

Customer records, medical details, passwords, employee information, anything shared with you in confidence. Where your typing actually goes, and how to set one rule you will remember.

06

Your first real habit

Picking one repetitive task in your own week and turning it into something you do with AI every time, rather than a tool you remember once a month.
